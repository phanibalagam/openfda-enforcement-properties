## 1. Parse rules

Applied to `product_description` of every record with no `openfda.product_ndc` (14,739 records).

ACCEPT:

- Hyphenated `\d{4,5}-\d{3,4}-\d{1,2}` with segment lengths exactly 4-4-2, 5-3-2, 5-4-1 or 5-4-2.
- Unhyphenated runs of exactly 10 or 11 digits, ONLY when the label is NDC.
- No candidate may touch a digit or hyphen on either side, so nothing is cut out of a longer hyphenated or digit run.
- Label = the last of these keywords within 40 characters before the candidate: ndc, ndc/upc, upc/ndc, upc, gtin, barcode, bar code, lot, exp, batch, sku, item, cat, catalog, serial, mfg, model. ndc/upc and upc/ndc count as NDC.

REJECT:

- any other segment shape (4-3-2, 5-3-1, 4-4-1, 4-3-1 ...);
- a candidate labeled upc, gtin, barcode, lot, exp, batch, sku, item, cat, catalog, serial, mfg or model (UPCs, lot numbers, expiry dates, catalog numbers);
- an unhyphenated 10 or 11 digit run with no NDC label (UPC and barcode digits);
- an all-zero labeler or product segment;
- a 4-4-2 whose first segment is 1990-2039 without an NDC label (date-like);
- quantities are excluded by construction: no accepted shape is a count, a decimal or a unit.

Normalization for matching: hyphenated forms are padded to 11 digits (4-4-2 pads the labeler, 5-3-2 the product, 5-4-1 the package); 5-4-2 and 11-digit forms are used as is. The first 9 digits are the product key, compared with openfda `product_ndc` padded the same way. A 10-digit unhyphenated code has three possible splits; it matches if any split does.

## 8. Notes

- **How the parse rules were set.** From counts of candidate shapes and of the label word preceding each candidate in the no-openfda records, before the gate was computed. No rule was changed after C1-C3 were seen. One code fix preceded the first complete run: 243 openfda `package_ndc` values that are not three hyphenated segments crashed the normalizer and are now skipped (they affect only the package-level corroboration row).
- **What the FAIL is.** The parse works (precision above, 99.3% of the "6002" yield an accepted code) and lifts pooled coverage well past 35%, but it does not flatten the curve: combined coverage is 29.85% in 2013 and 21.83% in 2015 against 83.26% in 2024 and 82.92% in 2026, and still rank-correlates 0.8198 with year (openfda alone: 0.9121). The parse adds records in every year but does not offset the rise in openfda coverage; why 2013 and 2015 recover so little was not investigated (out of scope for this run).
- **Low corroboration is expected, and is not the evidence.** Only 2.44% of distinct parsed codes match a product openfda assigned elsewhere. The recovered records mostly name products openfda never annotated, which is the gap by definition. The hand precision is the evidence that the parse reads real NDCs.
- **The criterion is not disputed.** C1 passes; C2 and C3 fail independently of each other, and no reasonable re-reading of either would change the verdict.
- **Boundary.** Snippets are quoted verbatim from public FDA records; the employer-boundary redaction ran over all 30 and replaced 0 strings. `boundary_scan.py` covers this report.
